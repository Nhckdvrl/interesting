# Main line: what an initialisation *is*, and which optimizer can see it

> One sentence: neural-network parameterisations carry exact reparameterisation
> symmetries, and **which of those symmetries the optimizer respects decides
> what an initialisation is** -- SGD respects them all and can only see
> invariants, AdamW respects only signed permutations and can also see the
> *frame*, so every frame is a free degree of freedom that the initialisation
> literature has been setting by accident.

## 1. The question

LoRA writes `Delta W = s B A`. A large literature proposes ways to choose `A`
(and sometimes `B`) at initialisation -- NoRA, ETF/BiMI, PiSSA, OLoRA, EVA,
gradient-subspace, LoRA-One -- and compares them on downstream loss. We asked
what those proposals are actually varying. The answer is not the one any of
them states.

## 2. The two symmetry groups

Two exact symmetries act on this setup.

**The adapter gauge, `O(r)`:** `(A, B) -> (QA, BQ^T)` leaves `BA` unchanged.
**The backbone gauge, `O(d)`:** `x -> Rx`, `W -> W R^T` leaves the function
unchanged (after folding the RMSNorm gains; see topic 02).

Both are exact. What differs is the optimizer.

* **SGD is exactly covariant under both.** For the adapter gauge,
  `dB = -eta s G A^T -> dB Q^T` and `dA = -eta s B^T G -> Q dA`, so the entire
  trajectory maps by the gauge -- with momentum, weight decay and global-norm
  clipping, all of which depend only on Frobenius norms. Two gauge-related
  initialisations are *the same run in different coordinates*.
* **AdamW is not.** Its second moment is elementwise, so it is steepest descent
  in the elementwise `l_inf` geometry, whose symmetry group is the signed
  permutations -- a discrete subgroup of `O(r)`.

So the state of an initialisation splits in two:

| part | what it is | who can see it |
|---|---|---|
| **invariants** | the `O(r)`-conjugacy class of the metric triple `M_0 = A A^T`, `M_x = A Sigma A^T`, `M_g = A C_g A^T` | SGD and AdamW |
| **frame** | the point on the orbit; `r(r-1)/2` degrees of freedom | AdamW only |

For the invariant part we give a complete order-`<=2` basis. Classical
invariant theory (Sibirskii, Procesi) says simultaneous `O(r)` invariants are
generated by traces of words, so at order `<= 2` there are exactly nine:
`tr M_0, tr M_x, tr M_g`, `tr M_0^2, tr M_x^2, tr M_g^2`, and the three cross
terms `tr M_0 M_x, tr M_0 M_g, tr M_x M_g`. Our earlier coordinates
(`S`, `D`, `omega`, `R_g`) use four of the nine. Verified numerically:
`D_*` and `Psi_*` are invariant under `O(r)` to 4e-16, while the frame
coordinates move under `O(r)` and are fixed under signed permutations to
4e-16 -- the exact group fingerprint.

**Schur-Horn ties the two together.** The reachable diagonals of `Q M Q^T` are
exactly those majorised by `eig(M)`, so the frame's range is *bounded by* the
invariants without being *determined* by them.

## 3. The measurement: the literature varies the frame by 4.3x, unknowingly

Measured with no training on 23 published initializer configurations
(`results/second_order.json`), using
`Lambda_1 = ||G A^T||_1^2 / (d_out r ||G A^T||_F^2)`, the exact ratio of
AdamW's first-order descent rate to SGD's:

| | `Lambda_1` |
|---|---|
| LoRA-One | 0.086 |
| gradient subspace | 0.152 |
| PiSSA | 0.273 |
| EVA | 0.285 |
| OLoRA | 0.294 |
| BiMI | 0.328 |
| ETF | 0.357 |
| NoRA | 0.366 |
| Kaiming | 0.366 |

The ordering is systematic: **data-aware methods sit low, frame-based methods
sit high**, and the whole NoRA family sits exactly at vanilla's value -- which
is why we measure those conditions as mutually indistinguishable to within
1.2x the measurement null. Published comparisons between these methods are, in
part, comparisons of an unreported coordinate.

## 4. The causal test (`results/frame`, 42 runs, pre-registered)

Predictions were committed in `01-hidden-preconditioner/PREDICTIONS_frame.md`
*before* the panel ran. Six initialisations on one gauge orbit,
`A = Q(t) A_0`, with `Lambda_1` spanning 2.9x (0.144 -> 0.422). Every logged
preconditioner statistic -- `tr_P`, `frob_P`, `crosstalk`, `eff_rank`,
`spec_max/min`, `diag_imbalance`, `colnorm_cv`, and crucially `tr_P_act` (= S)
and `tr_P_grad` (= `tr M_g`) -- equal to **1.5e-8** relative.

**SGD (prediction confirmed, exactly):**

```
lr = 0.03   0.47820  0.47819  0.47821  0.47819   ... kaiming 0.47820
lr = 0.1    0.46642  0.46643  0.46643  0.46643   ... kaiming 0.46642
```

Spread **1e-5 nats**. These are one trajectory in rotated coordinates. Even the
diverged `lr = 0.3` runs agree to five figures.

**AdamW, monotone at every learning rate:**

```
lr = 1e-4   0.45049 0.45108 0.45185 0.45205 0.45218   spread 0.00169
lr = 2e-4   0.44428 0.44510 0.44563 0.44600 0.44647   spread 0.00219
```

Tuned spread **0.00222 nats = 8.2x the measurement null**, against SGD's
0.00001. **The same intervention costs 222x more under AdamW than under SGD.**

Rotating the vanilla Kaiming draw to the gradient-metric eigenframe improves it
from 0.44551 to 0.44329 -- free, no parameters, no optimizer state, `BA` and
`P` preserved to 1e-15.

### How big is this, next to what those papers compare?

The six published initialisers span **0.01177 nats** between them, which is the
quantity their tables are about. The frame alone moves **one** method by
0.00204 nats (median) and 0.00594 (max) -- **17% to 51% of that entire range**,
and **2.5x the median gap between adjacent methods in the ranking** (0.00081).
It preserves `BA`, `P = s^2 A^T A` and all nine gauge invariants to 1e-15, and
no paper reports it.

### The pre-registered sign is wrong, and the mechanism is still open

Prediction 2 said higher `Lambda_1` should train *better*, because
`||grad_B||_1` is AdamW's first-order descent rate. The measurement says the
opposite at every learning rate: **concentrating gradient energy across the
adapter rows wins.** The `l_inf` argument identifies the right variable and the
right magnitude, and the wrong sign, so it is not what governs the 300-step
outcome.

A second pre-registration (`PREDICTIONS_mechanism.md`) proposed that AdamW,
being a *diagonal* preconditioner, should prefer the frame that diagonalises
the adapter-side curvature `M_x = A Sigma A^T`. That is falsified too:
`framex0` sets `Off_x` to exactly zero and is **not** the best frame (0.44542,
tied with vanilla), and within the orbit `Off_x` is uncorrelated with loss
(r = -0.004).

What survives is that AdamW's second moment estimates *gradient* variance, so
the relevant matrix is `M_g`, not `M_x`. Within the orbit
`Off_g` scores r = +0.859, `Lambda_1` +0.814 and `E_g` +0.806, and all three are
extremal at the same frame, so **these designs cannot separate them** and we do
not claim one. `Off_g` was reached post-hoc; 8B is its first out-of-sample test.
The practical prescription is unaffected: all three name the gradient-metric
eigenframe.

### Rotating the zoo (`results/rot`, 96 runs)

Each published initialiser as published, rotated to the gradient-metric
eigenframe, and rotated to a flat gradient-metric diagonal:

| method | published | `@frame0` | delta | `@frame1` | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kaiming | 0.44515 | **0.44322** | -0.00192 | 0.44495 | -0.00019 |
| bimi | 0.44556 | **0.44516** | -0.00041 | 0.44617 | +0.00061 |
| pissa | 0.45655 | **0.45198** | -0.00456 | 0.45790 | +0.00136 |
| eva | 0.45538 | **0.45459** | -0.00079 | 0.45675 | +0.00137 |
| gradsub | 0.45457 | 0.45478 | +0.00021 | 0.45526 | +0.00068 |
| lora_one | 0.45691 | 0.45676 | -0.00015 | 0.46271 | +0.00579 |

`@frame0` helps or is neutral **6/6**; `@frame1` hurts or is neutral **6/6**.
LoRA-One and gradsub are *already* at the eigenframe, so `@frame0` is the
identity there -- and those two cells give an unplanned reproducibility floor of
about 2e-4 nats for a 300-step run.

The headline pair survives three seeds (-0.00222, -0.00241, -0.00147; 3/3) and
a finer grid that rules out a learning-rate shift: both conditions peak at the
same lr = 3e-4 and `frame0`'s optimum is 0.0022 lower, so the rotation buys a
better optimum rather than a rescaled one.

## 5. What is running

* **`results/q8b`** -- Qwen3-8B, with predictions committed in
  `paper/8b_predictions.md` beforehand, including the unflattering P1 that the
  effect should **not** grow (the training-free frame reach is 4.57x at both
  0.6B and 1.7B).
* **`results/dolly_frame`, `results/rank` (r = 128)** -- a second,
  non-mathematical task, and the rank range people actually deploy.
* **`results/long`, `results/acc`** -- 1000 steps instead of 300, and GSM8K
  exact-match rather than nats. An initialisation effect that washes out by
  convergence is a curiosity; one that persists and moves accuracy is a
  prescription. Either answer goes in.
* **`02-.../results/muon_dose`** -- Muon on the *backbone* gauge, completing a
  3 optimizers x 2 symmetry groups table.

## 6. Relation to topic 02

Topic 02 ran the *backbone* gauge `O(d)` as a dose ladder
(`perm -> block4/16/64/256 -> random/hadamard`) and found the same split: SGD
flat, AdamW monotone, with FullFT 4.5x more gauge-sensitive than LoRA. That is
this section's statement one level up. The two topics are one principle at two
symmetry groups, which is why they belong in one paper.

## 7. Positioning

See `paper/POSITION.md`. Both halves of the argument are individually known --
Adam's failure of rotation-equivariance, and LoRA's gauge freedom (LoRA-RITE,
Balanced LoRA, FedRot-LoRA). All three of those treat the gauge as a defect to
eliminate. We invert it: the frame is a free, prescribable coordinate of the
*initialisation*, the zoo already sets it by accident, and LoRA-RITE's 7-point
GSM8K gain from a gauge-invariant optimizer becomes evidence for the mechanism
rather than competition.

## 8. Superseded by measurement

* `Lambda_1` as a *directional* prescription (falsified in sign, section 4).
* The unweighted captured-energy statistic `rho` (replaced by `R_g`).
* The pre-registered crosstalk statistic (killed analytically at Gate 0:
  `c(P)^2 = 1 - sum d^2 / sum lambda^2` is pinned in `[sqrt(1-r/d_in), 1]`).
* `(S, D, omega)` as sufficient (the OOD residual is real and the frame is at
  least part of what is missing).
