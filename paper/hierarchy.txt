Signed permutations are EXACTLY invariant for every optimizer here,
so that column is each optimizer's own float-noise floor at its own
learning rate -- which is what a chaotic trajectory near the
stability edge inflates.  The O(r) column is reported as a RATIO to
that floor, so the comparison is not contaminated by how close an
optimizer runs to divergence.

(An `inf` in the GL(r) column means the transformed run diverged where
 the original did not -- non-invariance in its loudest form, not a bug.)

Read the O(r)/floor column: methods with no preconditioner, an
orthogonalised one, or a full matrix on the rank index sit at ~1
(indistinguishable from their own float noise); diagonal methods sit
many orders above it.

The split is not adaptivity and not the norm.  It is whether the
preconditioner is DIAGONAL in the coordinates the gauge acts on:
  no preconditioner (SGD, SGD+m), orthogonalised (Muon), or a
  full matrix on the rank index  ->  blind to O(r)
  diagonal (AdamW, Lion, RMSprop, Adagrad, Adadelta)  ->  sees it

Every optimizer is blind inside its group and sensitive outside it:
  signed perms  <  O(r)  <  GL(r)
     AdamW         SGD       LoRA-RITE
                   Muon      Riemannion
                   matrix-precond Adam

So what an initialisation IS -- how many degrees of freedom it has
-- depends on which optimizer will consume it.  AdamW is the one
with the smallest symmetry group, and the one everybody uses.
